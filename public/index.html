01 / THE WALLET

Less between you
and your Lattice.

The essentials for using Lattice, with the decisions left in your hands. These capabilities describe the Selfhold release candidate; the live store version may differ.

[01]

Keys stay local.

Create a wallet or import a recovery phrase or private key. The encrypted vault stays in your browser profile; transactions are built and signed on your device.

[02]

Your node. Your choice.

Choose a hosted Lattice service or pair with your own node. Switch between Nexus and child chains, with connection settings saved per chain.

[03]

Review, then sign.

Check the recipient, amount, chain and fee before sending. Set your own fee and see when it is below your selected node’s relay minimum.

[04]

Keep a way back.

Export encrypted backups as files or QR codes. Retained transaction records help you check or resubmit an uncertain send without creating a second payment.

03 / GET STARTED

Three steps.
One responsibility.

Self-custody means you keep access to your wallet. Take a moment to make a backup before you use it.

  1. 01

    Check the listing.

    Open the existing Chrome Web Store listing on desktop Chrome. It currently uses the Lattice Wallet name. Review its permissions before installing.

  2. 02

    Create. Back up. Keep it private.

    Create a wallet and choose a password, or import an existing wallet. Store your recovery phrase offline and never share it with anyone, including support.

  3. 03

    Choose where you connect.

    Select your chain and a hosted or personal node. Check the full recipient address, amount and fee before your first send. Confirmation depends on the network and node operators.

↳
Your backup matters.

There is no publisher password reset for your keys. A recovery phrase alone does not restore imported keys or all local transaction recovery records. Keep an encrypted wallet backup, too.

04 / GOOD TO KNOW

A few clear answers.

Is Selfhold the same as Lattice Wallet?

Yes. Selfhold is the new name for Lattice Wallet and uses the same Chrome Web Store item. The live listing is still Lattice Wallet 0.3.1. Selfhold 0.4.1 has been uploaded as a draft and is awaiting final submission; it is not yet live or pending review.

Who controls my keys?

You do. The wallet creates or imports keys locally and stores them in an encrypted vault. It signs on your device. Protect your browser profile, password and backups; self-custody still requires care.

What does my chosen node see?

Node requests can disclose your IP address, selected chain, queried addresses and transaction identifiers. Approved transactions go to the selected node and may become public blockchain records. Read the wallet privacy policy for the details.

Is the code open source?

Yes. The wallet is available under the MIT license. You can read the source and build instructions. Open source makes inspection possible; it is not a guarantee of safety or an independent audit.

Where can I get help?

Contact support@lattice.build or open a GitHub issue. For vulnerabilities, follow the security reporting instructions. Never send recovery phrases, private keys, passwords or unredacted backups.
